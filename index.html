    About the artist

    Kavita Jha is a Mumbai-born artist who refuses to settle on a single material.

    Watercolour and terracotta, glazed ceramics and hand-painted cloth, sand drawn in light, the digital tablet, and installations large enough to walk through — the work moves freely across all of them, held together by a recurring cast of faces, gods, fish and many watching eyes.

    What follows is a journey through that body of work, grouped by medium. Take it slowly, top to bottom.

    I

    Chapter I

    Watercolour

    Pigment let loose on wet paper — woods, weathered houses and temple stone, each caught in a single breath.

    II

    Chapter II

    Portraits

    Faces of Indian cinema and art, built from translucent washes and a few certain strokes.

    III

    Chapter III

    Terracotta

    Earth pressed, scored and fired — masks, idols, and a wall panel that seems to remember.

    IV

    Chapter IV

    Ceramics

    Glaze and kiln-heat turned playful: tiles that echo Harappa, and teapots you can actually pour.

    V

    Chapter V

    Fabric-a

    The canvas becomes cloth — sarees and skirts painted by hand, made to be worn and walked in.

    VI

    Chapter VI

    Sand Art

    Drawn in light and loose grain — alive for a moment, then wiped away by the same hand that made it.

    VII

    Chapter VII

    Abstract

    Crowded, contradictory and full of eyes — feeling given a shape before it has time to settle.

    VIII

    Chapter VIII

    Exhibits

    The large statements — a Mumbai local, a national budget, a tribe. India read back to itself.

    IX

    Chapter IX

    Stage Décor

    Whole worlds built to be stood inside — Greek columns, a silver anniversary, a goddess of wisdom.

    X

    Chapter X

    Live Size

    Art you walk through — a tiger's open jaws, a hand holding a lit idea, the weight of space.

    XI

    Chapter XI

    Digital Art

    The newest room. Drawn on an iPad — the old motifs return in electric colour.

    XII

    Epilogue

    Workshops

    And the part that outlasts any single piece — putting the brush into someone else's hand.
